Learning Quadruped Walking from
Seconds of Demonstration

Ruipeng Zhang, Hongzhan Yu, Ya-Chien Chang, Chenghao Li, Henrik I. Christensen, Sicun Gao
University of California, San Diego
Paper arXiv Video Code

Abstract

Quadruped locomotion provides a natural setting for understanding when model-free learning can outperform model-based control design, by exploiting data patterns to bypass the difficulty of optimizing over discrete contacts and the combinatorial explosion of mode changes. We give a principled analysis of why imitation learning with quadrupeds can be inherently effective in a small data regime, based on the structure of limit cycles, Poincar'e return maps, and local numerical properties of neural networks. The understanding motivates a new imitation learning method that regularizes the alignment of distributions in a latent representation space with the output action variations. Hardware experiments confirm that a few seconds of demonstration is sufficient to train various locomotion policies from scratch entirely offline with reasonable robustness.

Methodology

[Insert Architecture Diagram Here]

Our approach utilizes a Hierarchical Latent Mapper. Instead of learning joint torques directly, we learn to navigate a manifold of stable gaits. This reduces the search space significantly, allowing the robot to "match" the human's demonstration in real-time.
